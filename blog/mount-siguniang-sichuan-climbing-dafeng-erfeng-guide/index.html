Mount Siguniang (Sichuan): independent logistics for Dafeng (5,025m) & Erfeng (5,276m)

Practical Sichuan mountaineering logistics for climbing Dafeng (5,025 m) and Erfeng (5,276 m) without overpaying an international tour broker: Chadianzi bus rules, Sichuan Mountaineering Association registration, Haizigou horse rates, summit ridge differences and managing the 500 m to 5,276 m altitude jump.

Mount Siguniang (Four Sisters Mountain) in western Sichuan offers two of Asia's most accessible 5,000-metre summits: Dafeng (First Peak, 5,025 m) and Erfeng (Second Peak, 5,276 m). You do not need USD 2,000 international expedition brokers to climb either peak, yet you cannot walk past the Haizigou valley gate unassisted either. Every ascent above 3,500 metres in Sichuan requires formal climbing registration, a licensed local guide link, and careful management of an aggressive elevation jump.

Chengdu sits at roughly 500 metres, while the trailhead town of Siguniangshan (formerly Rilong, ~3,100–3,200 m) sits over 2,600 metres higher just four to five hours away by road. This guide breaks down the exact independent logistics: public bus rules from Chengdu Chadianzi, Sichuan Mountaineering Association (SMA) permit channels, Haizigou valley distances and packhorse rates, terrain differences between Dafeng and Erfeng, and the best seasonal windows.

01Permits & local compliance

1. Sichuan Mountaineering Association registration, local guides & real CNY costs

Mandatory rulePrior registration required for all Sichuan peaks above 3,500 m
Baseline permit tier~300 CNY (Dafeng) / ~500 CNY (Erfeng) + 60 CNY Haizigou entry
Local 3-day package1,800–2,500 CNY/person via licensed Chinese outdoor clubs
Watch out forDirect individual applications are generally rejected; foreign passports need 10–20 days lead time

You cannot rock up to the Haizigou (Haizi Valley) ticket booth with a bare passport, buy a 60 CNY valley entry ticket, and hike independently to 5,025 metres. Under Sichuan provincial mountaineering regulations, ascents above 3,500 metres must be registered through local administrative authorities and the Sichuan Mountaineering Association (SMA). Because individual climbers cannot submit direct paperwork, registration must pass through an authorised local outdoor club, guide cooperative in Siguniangshan Town, or licensed agency.

If you book directly with a registered local club in Chengdu or Rilong rather than an overseas expedition reseller charging USD 500 to USD 2,000+, standard 3-day group packages typically price between 1,800 and 2,500 CNY per person. That rate bundles the Haizigou ticket, outdoor climbing permit, high-altitude rescue insurance, stone-hut or tent space at Base Camp, meals, group packhorses, and a local guide (often 1:1 or 1:2 on summit morning). Submit passport scans 10 to 20 days ahead so your paperwork clears before you arrive at the Dajianbao checkpoint.

Bottom lineBook registration and a local cooperative guide 10 to 20 days out through a licensed Sichuan club for 1,800–2,500 CNY instead of paying overseas broker mark-ups.
02Road transit from Chengdu

2. Chengdu Chadianzi buses, private cars & the oxygen-canister scanner rule

Bus terminalChengdu Chadianzi Bus Station (Metro Line 2, Exit A)
Schedule & fare~07:30–15:40 departures (buy Xiaojin bus); ~90–120 CNY one-way
Journey duration4 to 5 hours (~200 km over Balang Mountain Pass)
Watch out forOxygen canisters are banned through Chadianzi security scanners; buy them in Rilong

Public transit starts at Chengdu Chadianzi Bus Station beside Metro Line 2 Exit A. Many schedules list Xiaojin County as the final terminus rather than Siguniangshan Town (still commonly called Rilong by drivers), so buy a Xiaojin ticket at the counter using your physical passport and confirm you are alighting at Siguniangshan Town. Buses run across the morning and early afternoon from roughly 07:30 until 15:00–15:40, with higher frequency between April and November, and cost around 90 to 120 CNY.

Alternatively, 7-seater shuttle vans from central Chengdu (around Chunxi Road or Chadianzi) save a metro transfer if you carry bulky mountaineering duffels. One practical catch catches first-timers out every week: Chadianzi Bus Station X-ray scanners confiscate pressurised portable oxygen canisters. Never buy oxygen in Chengdu before boarding the coach; pharmacies and outdoor shops along Rilong's main street stock canisters and rental gaiters at standard local prices once you arrive.

Bottom lineTake an early morning Xiaojin-bound bus from Chadianzi with your physical passport, and save any oxygen or fuel purchases until you reach Rilong.
03Valley approach & packhorses

3. Haizigou valley stages, Base Camp altitudes & local horse rates

Trailhead to BC~13–16 km one-way from town; +1,000–1,100 m gain (5–7 hours)
Key checkpointDajianbao Protection Station (~7–7.5 km into Haizigou)
Base Camp elevation~4,200–4,300 m (simple stone huts, timber bunks & tents)
Watch out forRiding a horse all the way up skips active acclimatisation and leaves you stiff

Unlike Mount Siguniang's Changpinggou and Shuangqiaogou valleys, Haizigou has no sightseeing shuttle buses. You step off the pavement in Siguniangshan Town (~3,100–3,200 m) and walk the entire 13 to 16 km approach on foot alongside packhorses. The first 7 km climbs wooden boardwalks and open alpine pasture to Dajianbao Protection Station, where staff check your permit and outdoor registration before letting you continue another 6 to 7 km across boggy yak meadows and glacial moraine to Base Camp at 4,200 to 4,300 metres.

Local horsemen cooperatives operate standardised luggage and riding tariffs along this corridor: expect roughly 500 to 600 CNY per horse per day to haul expedition duffels, or 400 to 600+ CNY for a personal riding horse depending on the stage. Walking light with trekking poles while packhorses carry your sleeping bag and spare layers is far better for acclimatisation than sitting in a saddle for five hours, reserving riding horses strictly as an emergency descent option if altitude sickness hits.

Bottom linePay for packhorse duffel haulage if carrying heavy camera or winter kit, but walk the 13–16 km approach yourself to acclimatise properly.
04Choosing your summit

4. Dafeng (5,025 m) vs. Erfeng (5,276 m): terrain, crampons & ridge exposure

Dafeng profile5,025 m; ~24–34 km round-trip; broad scree & dirt walk-up
Erfeng profile5,276 m; ~5 km from BC to summit; steeper final rocky ridge
Hardware neededDafeng: poles + seasonal microspikes; Erfeng: crampons & occasional handlines
Watch out forErfeng's summit block holds wind-scoured blue ice and sharper exposure than Dafeng

Dafeng (First Peak, 5,025 m) earns its reputation as China's most approachable 5,000-metre summit because the line above Base Camp stays wide, non-technical, and easy to follow by head torch in 3 to 4 hours. Aside from loose scree and seasonal snow patches where microspikes or strap-on crampons keep your footing tidy before dawn, there are no exposed knife-edges or fixed-rope bottlenecks. If 5,000 metres is new territory for you, Dafeng lets you focus purely on breathing cadence and hydration.

Erfeng (Second Peak, 5,276 m) adds 251 vertical metres, heavier wind exposure, and a noticeably steeper final third. Starting from its ~4,300 m Base Camp around 02:00–03:00, you grind ~5 km up long scree switchbacks onto an upper ridge where mountain winds scour snow into slick crust. Proper 10- or 12-point crampons are standard here, and guides sometimes rig temporary handlines across short rocky steps near the top. It remains far less technical than Third Peak (Sanfeng) or main peak Yaomei (6,250 m), yet it punishes tired legs much harder than Dafeng.

Bottom linePick Dafeng for a reliable first 5,000 m benchmark; choose Erfeng if you want steeper crampon travel and have already handled 4,500 m+ cleanly.
05Managing the 4,776m climb

5. Surviving the Chengdu (500 m) to Base Camp (4,300 m) jump without AMS

Day 1 elevation shock+2,600–2,700 m road climb from Chengdu (500 m) to Rilong (~3,200 m)
Standard 3-day trapSleeping at 4,300 m on Night 2 leaves zero margin for slow acclimatisers
Smart 4-day fixAdd 1 rest/hike day in Rilong (~3,200 m) before entering Haizigou
Watch out forSkipping the extra night in Rilong causes most summit-morning headaches & nausea

Most commercial 3-day schedules compress the climb into a punishing physiological timetable: drive from Chengdu (500 m) to Rilong (3,100–3,200 m) on Day 1, hike straight to Base Camp (4,200–4,300 m) on Day 2, and start the 02:30 summit push to 5,025 m or 5,276 m on Day 3. For athletes arriving straight off flights without recent altitude exposure, jumping nearly 3,800 vertical metres in sleeping elevation inside 36 hours triggers acute mountain sickness (AMS) before summit morning even starts.

If your calendar allows one extra day, turning a 3-day sprint into a 4-day itinerary transforms the climb. Spend two nights in Siguniangshan Town at ~3,200 m, using your spare day for a relaxed 3-to-4-hour walk along the paved boardwalks of Shuangqiaogou or Changpinggou without heavy pack strain. Sleeping low a second night clears the initial Balang Pass headache, preserves your appetite at Base Camp, and raises your summit odds dramatically.

Bottom lineSpend two nights in Siguniangshan Town (~3,200 m) before sleeping at 4,300 m Base Camp; that single extra day prevents most AMS turnarounds.
06Weather windows & gear

6. Autumn vs. spring conditions, Golden Week traps & 5,000m summit kit

Prime autumn windowLate September to early November (mid-Oct peak clarity)
Spring windowMay to June (rhododendrons below, firmer snow above)
Summit night temp-10°C to -15°C with ridge windchill (even colder Dec–Feb)
Watch out forChina's Oct 1–7 National Day Golden Week doubles town hotel rates & crowds

Late September through early November delivers the cleanest mountain visibility in western Sichuan once the summer monsoon clears. Daytime sun in Haizigou feels mild while golden larch forests light up the valley floor, though overnight lows at Base Camp still freeze water bottles solid. Just avoid China's October 1–7 National Day Golden Week, when Chadianzi buses sell out days early and basic 150–300 CNY guesthouses in Rilong triple their tariffs. May and June form the second reliable climbing window before July–August monsoon storms soak the valley.

Pack as you would for a cold 4,500-metre Alpine hut start rather than a warm summer trek. You need stiff B1 or B2 waterproof boots with ankle support for frozen mud and scree, gaiters, thermal merino base layers, an active grid fleece, a windproof hardshell, an 800-fill down belay jacket for summit rests, warm leather-palm gloves, Category 3 or 4 glacier sunglasses, and an insulated wide-mouth bottle that stays liquid when bite valves freeze.

Bottom lineTarget mid-October or late May for clear summit skies, skip October 1–7 Golden Week, and pack proper sub-zero summit layers.

Treat Dafeng and Erfeng with the same respect you would give a 4,000-metre Alpine ridge: pace the first 7 km out of Rilong slow enough to hold a conversation, drink 3 to 4 litres of fluids daily before reaching Base Camp, and keep a dry spare pair of socks plus your head-torch batteries inside an inner jacket pocket overnight.

Frequently asked questions

Can foreigners climb Mount Siguniang Dafeng or Erfeng completely solo without a guide?

No. Sichuan provincial regulations require climbing registration and permits for peaks above 3,500 metres, and the Sichuan Mountaineering Association (SMA) does not accept direct individual applications. Foreign climbers must register through an authorised local outdoor club or mountain guide cooperative 10 to 20 days in advance.

How much does it cost to climb Dafeng or Erfeng independently via a local Chinese club?

While bare baseline statutory registration tiers sit around 300 CNY for Dafeng and 500 CNY for Erfeng plus the 60 CNY Haizigou entrance ticket, most independent travellers join a local 3-day club package from Chengdu or Rilong for 1,800 to 2,500 CNY. That bundles permits, local guide support, Base Camp lodging and meals, packhorse duffel carriage, and insurance.

Should I climb Dafeng (5,025 m) or Erfeng (5,276 m) first?

Choose Dafeng if it is your first time above 4,500 metres or you want a straightforward, non-technical snow-and-scree walk-up. Choose Erfeng if you already acclimatise reliably, carry crampons comfortably, and want a steeper, more exposed alpine summit ridge.

Can I bring portable oxygen cans on the Chengdu Chadianzi bus to Siguniangshan Town?

No. Station X-ray security at Chengdu Chadianzi Bus Station prohibits pressurised oxygen canisters in both carry-on and stowed luggage. Wait until you arrive in Siguniangshan Town (Rilong), where local pharmacies and gear shops sell them openly.
