Cycling Japan end to end: route planning, rinkō train bags, tunnels & konbini fuelling

How to plan and ride the length of Japan between Cape Sata in Kyushu and Cape Soya in Hokkaidō: seasonal tsuyu rain timing, Sea of Japan vs. Pacific coastal corridors, Shinkansen rinkō bike bag dimensions, ferry and undersea tunnel crossings, konbini fuelling, hotel bike rules and Japan's 2026 blue-ticket cycling fines.

Riding the length of Japan from Cape Sata at the southern tip of Kyushu to Cape Soya on the northern coast of Hokkaidō (known locally as Nihon Juudan) is one of the smoothest long-distance road tours anywhere in the world. Having ridden Japan end to end from May into June 2026, the appeal comes down to density and predictability: clean tarmac, 24-hour convenience stores, roadside rest stations and hot spring baths appear on a rhythm almost nowhere else matches.

What catches visiting and resident riders off guard is rarely fitness. It is Japan's specific operational rules: which Shinkansen lines fine you ¥1,000 for an unreserved bike bag, how the tsuyu rainy season marches north across Kyushu and Honshu while sparing Hokkaidō, why narrow coastal tunnels demand serious daytime lighting, and how Japan's April 2026 blue-ticket bicycle fines apply on open roads.

01Route window

1. South-to-north timing & dodging the tsuyu rain front

Typical distance2,700–3,000 km direct; 3,300–3,700 km via scenic passes or Shikoku
Rainy season onsetLate May in southern Kyushu; early June in Honshu; none in Hokkaidō
Riding temperatures18–26°C across May Honshu; crisp sub-15°C mornings in northern Hokkaidō
Watch out forLate-May downpours in Kyushu and lingering May snow on high Hokkaidō passes

Most end-to-end riders start at Cape Sata in Kagoshima and pedal north to Cape Soya in Wakkanai rather than riding south. Leaving Kyushu in early to mid-May keeps you ahead of the brutal midsummer humidity and lets you track spring warmth northward: Honshu is dry and mild through May, and by the time the tsuyu rainy front settles over central Japan in early June, you are rolling across northern Tohoku toward Hokkaidō.

Hokkaidō has no official tsuyu season because the seasonal rain front normally stalls before crossing the Tsugaru Strait. That makes June prime cycling weather in the north, with long daylight hours and dry coastal tarmac. Riding the reverse north-to-south direction in spring forces you into cold northern starts in April or pushes you into Kyushu right as summer heat and typhoon season build.

Bottom lineStart in Kyushu in early to mid-May so you clear Honshu before deep tsuyu humidity and reach Hokkaidō in crisp June daylight.
02Honshu routing

2. Sea of Japan coast vs. Pacific corridor vs. the mountain spine

Sea of Japan (West)Fishing ports, rolling coastal headlands, lighter commercial freight
Pacific Coast (East)Flat urban stretches, endless konbini, heavy lorry volume near metros
Inland Mountain SpineNagano and Japanese Alps passes linking valleys between coasts
Watch out forLong, narrow-shoulder coastal tunnels along the San'in and Hokuriku cliffs

Honshu offers three distinct personalities between Yamaguchi and Aomori. The Sea of Japan coast (San'in, Hokuriku and Akita) delivers raw ocean cliffs, small harbour towns and far fewer articulated lorries than the Tokyo-Osaka industrial belt. By contrast, the Pacific side gives you fast, flat stretches and dense convenience-store coverage, though national arteries such as Route 1 carry relentless freight traffic unless you weave onto coastal bike paths or jump over Shikoku and the Shimanami Kaido bridges.

If you have climbing gears (at least a 1:1 low ratio for loaded touring), cutting diagonally through the inland mountain spine across Nagano, Gunma or Tohoku breaks up repetitive coastal tarmac with cool valley roads and hot-spring towns. Whichever corridor you favour, inspect tunnel density on your mapping app before locking a stage: fifty kilometres of coastal cliffs can hide a dozen dark tunnels in a single afternoon.

Bottom lineFavour the Sea of Japan coast or inland mountain valleys for calmer riding, reserving Pacific stretches for specific links such as the Shimanami Kaido.
03Rail & packing

3. Rinkō bike bags, JR Shinkansen rules & bailout logistics

Ostrich L-100 spec~250 g complete with straps (¥7,900–¥8,690 MSRP; rear end cap ¥1,750–¥2,640)
JR size ceilingTotal L+W+H up to 250 cm (max 200 cm length), max 30 kg, 100% enclosed
Line reservation splitFree oversized-baggage seat required >160 cm on Tokaido, Sanyo & Kyushu
Watch out for¥1,000 onboard penalty on Tokaido/Sanyo/Kyushu without a reservation; exposed wheels rejected

A rinkō-bukuro (lightweight nylon bike transit bag) is the single highest-value item in your frame bag. On every JR train, ferry and highway bus, an uncovered bicycle is refused at the ticket barrier; once both wheels are off, strapped to the frame and zipped inside a thin cover weighing roughly 250 grams, your bike legally becomes standard passenger baggage. Always carry the matching rear end-cap protector (sold separately in 130/135 mm quick-release or 12 mm thru-axle versions) so the bike stands vertically without bending your rear derailleur hanger.

Check which Shinkansen operator runs your line before boarding to dodge rain or fly home from Sapporo or Wakkanai. On the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen, bags whose length + width + height total between 161 cm and 250 cm require a free pre-booked Seat with an Oversized Baggage Area (at the back row of the car), or conductors charge a ¥1,000 penalty fee. On JR East lines north of Tokyo (Tohoku, Hokuriku, Joetsu and Hokkaido Shinkansen), no oversized-baggage reservation is needed.

Bottom linePack an Ostrich L-100 with a rear derailleur end cap so every JR local train, Shinkansen and urban hotel accepts your bike as luggage.
04Island crossings

4. Kanmon Pedestrian Tunnel, Tsugaru ferries & tunnel safety

Kanmon Tunnel780 m undersea link (Shimonoseki–Moji); open 06:00–22:00; ¥20 bike toll
Tsugaru Kaikyo FerryAomori–Hakodate ~3.5 h (¥1,250–¥1,800 bike); Oma–Hakodate ~90 min (¥840–¥1,190)
Tunnel lighting setup600+ lumen front beam + daytime-flash rear light + reflective ankle strap
Watch out forKanmon lifts close at 22:00 (pushing only, no riding); mountain tunnels with zero shoulder

Linking Japan's main islands requires two landmark crossings if you skip trains. Between Kyushu and Honshu, cyclists cannot ride across the high Kanmon Bridge; instead, you take an elevator down into the 780-metre Kanmon Pedestrian Tunnel connecting Moji and Shimonoseki. Open from 06:00 to 22:00 daily, it costs ¥20 for a bicycle (dropped into the coin box by the lift), and you must dismount and push your bike the whole way across the prefectural border under the seabed.

Crossing from Honshu into Hokkaidō means boarding a ferry across the Tsugaru Strait. On the Tsugaru Kaikyo Ferry, bicycle transport is charged separately from the passenger ticket as special baggage: roughly ¥1,250 to ¥1,800 on the 3.5-hour Aomori–Hakodate crossing depending on seasonal Period A/B/C tariffs, or ¥840 to ¥1,190 on the 90-minute Oma–Hakodate hop from Shimokita Peninsula. Elsewhere on Honshu, treat rural road tunnels with respect: switch your rear light on before every portal, wait for traffic gaps on long single-lane climbs, and use the raised maintenance kerb only when safe and swept clean.

Bottom lineTime your Shimonoseki crossing well before 22:00, book your Tsugaru ferry seasonal period online, and run bright front and rear lights all day.
05Daily resupply

5. Konbini & Michi-no-Eki fuelling (plus cash realities)

Konbini staple pricingOnigiri ¥170–¥235 tax-in; chilled coffee, sports drinks, bananas & hot meals 24/7
Michi-no-Eki network1,200+ roadside stations with 24h toilets, shaded benches, water & local kitchens
Cash bufferKeep ¥10,000–¥15,000 in notes + ¥100 coins for rural vending machines & tunnel tolls
Watch out for30–60 km zero-shop stretches along rural Hokkaidō coasts and mountain passes after 18:00

In most of Honshu and Kyushu, 7-Eleven, Lawson and FamilyMart eliminate the need to haul heavy stove fuel or three days of emergency food. Onigiri rice balls (¥170–¥235), boiled eggs, sweet bean buns, ice packs for hot climbs and 500 ml sports drinks from ubiquitous roadside vending machines let you fuel 150 km stages from two frame bottles and a top-tube pouch. Between towns, government-run Michi-no-Eki roadside stations provide clean 24-hour toilets, bike racks, maps and hot regional lunches.

That convenience evaporates abruptly once you enter northern Tohoku or coastal Hokkaidō. Rural fishing villages can run 40 to 60 kilometres without a convenience store, and family-run restaurants often close by 18:00 or shut randomly on weekdays. Always top up bottles and grab two spare onigiri whenever you leave a regional town in the afternoon, and keep ¥100 coins handy for vending machines, coin laundries and the Kanmon Tunnel toll.

Bottom lineRide light on food through Honshu's konbini network, then switch to disciplined 60 km reserve-calorie discipline once you cross into northern Tohoku and Hokkaidō.
06Sleep & legal compliance

6. Business hotels, onsen recovery & Japan's 2026 blue-ticket bicycle law

Business hotel baselineToyoko Inn ¥8,400–¥10,500 weeknights (free breakfast); Route Inn adds public baths
In-room bike policyAssembled bikes refused in standard rooms; bagged rinkō bikes pass as luggage
2026 Blue Ticket finesEffective 1 Apr 2026 (age 16+): ¥12,000 phone holding, ¥6,000 red light/wrong side, ¥5,000 stop/lights
Watch out forCriminal red-ticket penalties for any alcohol on a bike, and rural hotel coin laundries closing early

Standard Japanese business hotel chains such as Toyoko Inn and Hotel Route Inn make multi-week touring remarkably restful: clean rooms, free breakfast, coin laundry machines and, at most Route Inns or Dormy Inns, a hot communal bath to soak stiff quads before bed. However, standard chains strictly forbid rolling bare bicycles across carpeted corridors into bedrooms. Packing your frame into a clean rinkō bag takes five minutes in the car park and turns your bicycle into compliant luggage; alternatively, check Japan's Cyclist Welcome directory for properties with indoor racks.

On the road, respect Japan's tightened Road Traffic Act enforcement. Since 1 April 2026, police issue on-the-spot Blue Tickets (ao-kippu) with fixed administrative fines to cyclists aged 16 and older: ¥12,000 for holding or staring at a mobile phone while riding, ¥6,000 for running a red light or riding on the right-hand side against traffic, and ¥5,000 for rolling through a Tomare stop sign, riding unlit at night or wearing headphones that block traffic sound. Cycling after drinking alcohol sits outside the blue-ticket tier altogether as a severe criminal offence.

Bottom lineBag your bike in a rinkō cover for stress-free hotel rooms, obey left-side road and Tomare stop signs strictly, and keep your phone locked on a mount rather than in your hand.

Before shipping or flying your bike to Kagoshima or Hokkaido, run one full timed rinkō drill in your living room. Practice removing both wheels, seating the rear end-cap protector, strapping the wheels to the chainstays without rubbing brake rotors, and tightening the shoulder strap through the frame triangle so the weight sits on the bottom bracket rather than the nylon fabric. Add one spare derailleur hanger, a quick-link, two tubes and brake pads to your saddle bag: rural Japanese towns have outstanding hospitality, but rarely stock specific Western road or gravel spares on the shelf.

Frequently asked questions

How long is cycling Japan end to end from Cape Sata to Cape Soya?

A direct road route between Cape Sata in southern Kyushu and Cape Soya in northern Hokkaidō covers roughly 2,700 to 3,000 km. Riders who add Shikoku, coastal peninsulas, or central Japanese Alps mountain passes typically log 3,300 to 3,700 km over three to five weeks.

Do you need a reservation for a bike in a rinkō bag on the Shinkansen?

It depends on the line and total bag dimensions. Your bike must be 100% enclosed in a rinkō bag, weigh 30 kg or less, and measure 250 cm or less in total length + width + height. If the packed bag measures 161–250 cm, you must book a free back-row Seat with an Oversized Baggage Area on the Tokaido, Sanyo, Kyushu and Nishi Kyushu Shinkansen (otherwise a ¥1,000 penalty applies). JR East Shinkansen lines north of Tokyo do not require this reservation.

Can you bring a bicycle inside a Toyoko Inn or Route Inn hotel room in Japan?

Standard Toyoko Inn and Hotel Route Inn branches prohibit bringing uncovered bicycles into guest rooms or hallways. However, if you pack the bike cleanly inside a zipped rinkō transit bag with no dirty wheels exposed, it is treated as ordinary passenger luggage, or you can book dedicated Cyclist Welcome hotels with indoor bike rooms.

What are the 2026 Japan bicycle traffic fines touring cyclists need to watch?

Under Japan's Blue Ticket (ao-kippu) system introduced on 1 April 2026 for cyclists aged 16 and older, police issue on-the-spot fines including ¥12,000 for holding a mobile phone while riding, ¥6,000 for riding on the wrong (right) side of the road or running a red light, and ¥5,000 for failing to stop at a Tomare stop sign, riding without lights at night, or wearing sound-blocking earphones. Cycling under the influence of alcohol remains a criminal offence carrying severe penalties.
