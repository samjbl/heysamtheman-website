Tokyo: 6 flat & hilly running loops with zero traffic lights (plus runner stations)

Where to run uninterrupted tempo blocks, hill repeats and 30 km marathon long runs in Tokyo without stopping at pedestrian crossings. Exact loop distances, elevation profiles, station access, shower and locker yen fees, and local etiquette caveats.

Road running in central Tokyo falls apart the moment you pick a standard city avenue. Even on wide boulevards, a pedestrian light every 250 metres ruins marathon-pace rhythm and forces constant acceleration from a standstill.

The six courses below eliminate traffic signals completely. Together they cover every block in a distance or mountain-conditioning plan: a measured 2,140-metre interval loop, a hilly 3.3 km palace wall for strength work, a shaded soft-surface park, a flat waterfront promenade and two river levees built for 30 km marathon long runs, alongside the exact locker and shower bases that make post-work or layover runs painless.

01Best central loop & runner-station network

1. Imperial Palace (Kokyo) 5 km Perimeter Loop

Station accessTakebashi, Otemachi, Hibiya, Sakuradamon or Tokyo Station (1 to 5 min walk)
Course & elevation4.97 km (~5.0 km) closed loop · +26–30 m gentle elevation change per lap · zero signals
Runner stationsRaffine Neo Hibiya (¥950) / Otemachi One (¥1,100), Re.Ra.Ku PRO Takebashi (~¥1,000), Marunouchi Bike & Run (¥1,200 + ¥1,000 initial card)
Watch out forStrictly anti-clockwise running etiquette; expect heavy foot traffic on weekday evenings (18:00–20:00) and cashless-only entry at Raffine

The moat perimeter around Tokyo's Imperial Palace remains the benchmark five-kilometre road loop in Japan for one reason: unbroken logistics. You step out of half a dozen subway exits straight onto a wide stone-and-asphalt path with zero road crossings, drinking fountains spaced cleanly along the moat and a gentle 30-metre climb past Chidorigafuchi up to Hanzomon Gate followed by a fast downhill glide into Sakuradamon.

Three serious runner stations sit steps from the tarmac. Raffine Running Style runs cashless drop-in access at Hibiya (¥950) and Otemachi One (¥1,100) from 07:00 to 22:00 on weekdays (until 20:00 weekends). Re.Ra.Ku PRO connects directly to Takebashi Station for around ¥1,000 per visit, while Marunouchi Bike & Run inside Shin-Marunouchi Building (¥1,200 per use after a one-off ¥1,000 card fee) opens earliest at 06:30 for pre-flight or Tokyo Station runs.

Bottom lineUnmatched transit and shower convenience in central Tokyo, provided you run anti-clockwise and avoid peak 18:30 commuter bottlenecks.
02Best central hill loop & low-crowd alternative

2. Akasaka Palace (State Guest House) 3.3 km Rolling Circuit

Station accessYotsuya, Shinanomachi, Akasaka-mitsuke or Aoyama-itchome (2 to 5 min walk)
Course & elevation~3.3 km perimeter loop · +30–33 m elevation per lap with two steep climbs · zero signals on the loop
Runner stationsNo dedicated run station on the wall; use station coin lockers or showers at nearby Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium (Sendagaya)
Watch out forApproach streets from Yotsuya have crossings, and diplomatic visits can narrow sections near the main State Guest House gates

Two kilometres west of the Imperial Palace, the tree-lined stone wall around the Akasaka Estate and State Guest House offers a quieter 3.3 km circuit with actual gradients. Unlike the smooth roll of Kokyo, Akasaka forces you up two crisp climbs (including Kinokuni-zaka and Anchin-zaka) every lap, making 4-to-6-lap blocks (13 to 20 km) far better preparation for hilly marathons and mountain descents.

Because the immediate footpath hugs the continuous estate wall, you run lap after lap without touching a pedestrian crossing. There is no purpose-built runner station directly on the perimeter, so pack light with a running belt or drop a small daypack into coin lockers inside Yotsuya or Shinanomachi Station before starting.

Bottom lineTokyo's sharpest central strength-endurance loop when you want real hills and half the Imperial Palace foot traffic.
03Best measured track for intervals & tempo

3. Komazawa Olympic Park 2,140 m Dedicated Running Course

Station accessKomazawa-daigaku Station (Tokyu Den-en-toshi Line, 12 min walk south)
Course & elevationExactly 2,140 m (2.14 km) flat loop · distance markers every 100 m · park open 24 hours
Runner stationsKomazawa Olympic Park Training Room (¥450 per 2h via vending machine) or JAM Runtrip BASE near West Exit
Watch out forKeep strictly to the marked anti-clockwise running lane next to the 2,086 m cycling path; the stadium gym closes 1 to 3 weekdays monthly

Built for the 1964 Summer Games, Komazawa Olympic Park in Setagaya removes every ounce of GPS drift from threshold sessions. Its outer asphalt circuit measures exactly 2,140 metres and carries painted distance markers every 100 metres, letting you run 1 km repeats, 10 km tempos or progressive marathon blocks purely on stopwatch splits.

Unlike multi-use city pavements, Komazawa physically separates pedestrians, runners and cyclists into parallel lanes around the stadium. For showers and lockers on a budget, head underneath the athletics stadium stands to the municipal Training Room (¥450 for a two-hour ticket, open 09:00–21:30 weekdays and 07:30–21:00 weekends; bring clean indoor shoes if stepping onto the gym floor), or use JAM Runtrip BASE just outside the park.

Bottom lineThe cleanest flat interval and tempo circuit in Tokyo, backed by ¥450 municipal shower access.
04Best shaded soft-surface recovery loop

4. Yoyogi Park Outer & Interior Tree-Canopy Loops

Station accessHarajuku (JR Yamanote), Meiji-jingumae or Yoyogi-koen Station (2 to 4 min walk)
Course & elevation1.8 km to 2.2 km flat circuits · mix of paved outer road and crushed dirt/gravel tracks · open 24h
Runner stationsRuntrip BASE Yoyogi Park (¥880 free app member / ¥1,100 visitor for lockers and showers)
Watch out forHeavy Sunday afternoon picnic and dog-walking traffic near the central fountain; humid mosquito season from June to September

When your calves are beaten up from concrete mileage or mountain descents, Yoyogi Park gives you Tokyo's best central soft-surface sanctuary. Hugging the perimeter beneath tall zelkova and ginkgo trees creates a flat 1.8 to 2.2 km circuit with generous stretches of packed dirt and fine gravel alongside the tarmac inner ring.

Run early before 08:30 on weekends to share the dirt lines almost exclusively with local track clubs and marathon groups. Five minutes from the park edge near Harajuku, Runtrip BASE Yoyogi Park handles bag drop, showers and post-run coffee for ¥1,100 (or ¥880 if you register on the free Runtrip app).

Bottom lineIdeal for shaded easy miles, softer joint recovery and club meetups steps from Shibuya and Harajuku.
05Best flat bayside run + sunrise onsen finish

5. Toyosu Gururi Park 4.5 km Waterfront Promenade

Station accessShijo-mae or Shin-Toyosu Station (Yurikamome Line, 3 to 5 min walk)
Course & elevation~4.5 km one-way coastal promenade (~9 km out-and-back) · completely flat · 400 m markers · zero signals
Runner stationsTokyo Toyosu Manyo Club at Shijo-mae (¥2,200 morning bath 06:00–09:00; ¥4,000+¥150 daytime — leave bag in locker with front-desk check)
Watch out forU-shaped pier layout means running out-and-back (closing the loop via street blocks hits traffic lights); strong bay winds and zero summer shade

Wrapping the perimeter of Toyosu Pier around the fish market, Toyosu Gururi Park delivers 4.5 kilometres of wide, uninterrupted bayside asphalt facing the Rainbow Bridge and central skyline. Painted 400-metre distance ticks make threshold pacing effortless, and because the path sits on reclaimed waterfront land, there is not a single road intersection or uphill kick anywhere along the pier.

The smart weekend play pairs a dawn 15–20 km out-and-back session with Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club beside Shijo-mae Station. Check in during the 06:00–09:00 morning bath window (¥2,200), stash your gear in their full-size lockers with reception informed, run empty-handed along the water and finish straight in open-air hot spring tubs overlooking Tokyo Bay.

Bottom lineUnbeatable dawn skyline views and threshold pacing, especially paired with Toyosu Manyo Club's ¥2,200 morning bath.
06Best 30 km+ marathon long-run corridors

6. Arakawa & Tamagawa River Paths (30 to 40+ km Uninterrupted)

Station accessArakawa: Akabane-Iwabuchi or Kita-Senju / Kasai Rinkai Park; Tamagawa: Futako-Tamagawa or Tamagawa Station
Course & elevation30 to 40+ km linear paved levees · completely flat (except short bridge ramps) · 1 km posts along Arakawa
Runner stationsSelf-supported: use station coin lockers at Futako-Tamagawa or Akabane, or finish at a local neighbourhood sento (¥550)
Watch out forZero wind shelter in winter and zero shade in summer; fast weekend road cyclists share the levee tarmac so hold a tight single-file line on the left

Once marathon training climbs past 25 kilometres, running ten laps of Yoyogi Park becomes a mental grind. Tokyo's two great river corridors solve that problem cleanly: the Arakawa River path stretches uninterrupted from Akabane-Iwabuchi down to Kasai Rinkai Park on Tokyo Bay with physical 1 km distance markers along the levee, while the Tamagawa River path runs more than 40 kilometres from western Tokyo through Futako-Tamagawa and Numabe almost to Haneda Airport.

Both rivers let you dial in 30 to 35 km progressive long runs without touching a single stoplight, and Tamagawa pairs wide tarmac with parallel packed-dirt floodplains that save your calves on high-mileage Sundays. Carry ¥550 in coins or a charged Suica for bridge-side vending machines and drop into a traditional neighbourhood sento near the return station to rinse off.

Bottom lineThe only truly signal-free 30 km+ marathon training corridors in Tokyo, best run early before weekend cyclists and afternoon headwinds peak.

Keep two Tokyo running habits in mind before heading out: carry a small zip pouch for gel wrappers because public street bins are nearly non-existent, and load a mobile Suica or IC card on your phone so station lockers, vending-machine sports drinks and cashless run stations like Raffine take two seconds with zero wallet weight.

Frequently asked questions

Which way do you run around the Imperial Palace (Kokyo) in Tokyo?

Always run anti-clockwise (counter-clockwise) around the Imperial Palace moat. It is a strictly observed unwritten rule followed by all local runners to avoid head-on collisions on narrower stone pavements near Takebashi and Chidorigafuchi.

Where can I store my bag and shower after running in central Tokyo?

Around the Imperial Palace, use Raffine Running Style (¥950 at Hibiya Neo, ¥1,100 at Otemachi One, cashless only), Re.Ra.Ku PRO Takebashi (~¥1,000), or Marunouchi Bike & Run at Tokyo Station (¥1,200 plus ¥1,000 first-time card fee). Near Yoyogi Park, use Runtrip BASE Yoyogi Park (¥880 app / ¥1,100 visitor); at Komazawa Olympic Park, the stadium Training Room charges ¥450 for 2 hours.

What is the best Tokyo running route for hills without traffic lights?

The 3.3 km outer perimeter loop around Akasaka Palace (State Guest House) is central Tokyo's best signal-free hill circuit. Each lap gains roughly 30 to 33 metres across two distinct climbs (including Kinokuni-zaka and Anchin-zaka) with far lighter foot traffic than the Imperial Palace.

Where do Tokyo marathon runners do 30 km long runs without stopping?

Serious Tokyo marathoners move out to the Arakawa River levee path (which has 1 km markers all the way toward Kasai Rinkai Park) or the 40+ km Tamagawa River cycling and running road through Futako-Tamagawa and Numabe, both offering 30 km+ of flat, traffic-light-free running.
