Tokyo: 6 best mountain day hikes under 2 hours by train (with trailhead onsen)

Six point-to-point ridges, steep training climbs and coastal cliff routes reachable in 50 to 115 minutes from Shinjuku or Shinagawa without a car. Every route finishes at a natural hot spring beside the return station or bus stop, with verified rail times, vertical metres, yen fees and closure days.

Living in central Tokyo makes weekday speedwork easy and mountain legs hard, unless you use the suburban rail network properly. Because JR East, Odakyu and Keio lines punch straight into the Chichibu-Tama-Kai and Tanzawa foothills, you do not need to hike out-and-back to a car park. You can step off one rural station at 07:30, traverse a 1,300-metre ridgeline, drop into a different valley for a hot mineral soak, and ride an evening express home in clean socks.

Most English hiking lists stop at paved tourist walks up Mt. Takao. The six routes below are chosen for hikers, trail runners and mountaineers who want real leg conditioning, clean transit connections under two hours one-way, and a reliable hot spring at the finish line.

01Best fast ridge traverse & trail-run classic

1. Mt. Jinba (855 m) to Mt. Takao (599 m) Ridge Traverse

Train & bus accessShinjuku → Takao Station (~50 min on Keio/JR), then 37 min bus from North Exit #1 to Jinba Kogen Shita
Distance & vertical18.5 km point-to-point · +880 m ascent · 5 to 6.5 hours hiking (2.5–3.5 hours running)
Finish-line onsenKeio Takaosan Onsen Gokurakuyu (08:00–22:30, ¥1,100 weekday / ¥1,300 weekend, connected to station)
Watch out forAlways hike west-to-east (Jinba → Takao) so you end at the train and onsen instead of waiting for a rural bus in the dark

Walking up Mt. Takao's paved Trail 1 on a Saturday feels like queueing at Shibuya Crossing. Start fifteen kilometres further west at Jinba Kogen Shita and you get a completely different mountain day: a quiet cedar climb onto the open grass summit of Mt. Jinba (with clear-morning views straight across to Mt. Fuji), followed by a rolling, runnable singletrack ridge over Kageyama and Kobotoke Shiroyama.

By the time you merge onto Takao's busy summit terrace in the afternoon, nine-tenths of your mileage is already banked. Skip the cable car, jog down Trail 6 or Inariyama, and walk straight into Keio Takaosan Onsen Gokurakuyu right beside Takaosanguchi Station. It runs outdoor carbonated stone baths and high-heat saunas until 22:30, with semi-express Keio trains departing steps from the locker room.

Bottom lineThe cleanest runnable 18 km ridge near Tokyo, with zero bus stress at the finish.
02Best forest shrine + rocky scramble loop

2. Mt. Mitake (929 m) & Mt. Odake (1,266 m) to Tsurutsuru Onsen

Train & bus accessJR Chuo/Ome Line from Shinjuku → Mitake Station (~90 min), 10 min bus to Mitake Tozan Cable
Distance & vertical12.5 km traverse · +650 m with cable car (+1,020 m from valley floor) · 4.5 to 5.5 hours
Finish-line onsenShogai Seishun no Yu Tsurutsuru Onsen (10:00–20:00, ¥1,050 weekday / ¥1,150 weekend for 3h)
Watch out forTsurutsuru Onsen closes on the 3rd Tuesday of each month; check the hourly locomotive-bus timetable to Musashi-Itsukaichi

One of Okutama's sharpest point-to-point days links the mossy gorges of Rock Garden (Nanayo-no-Taki and Ayahiro Falls) with the short chained rock steps onto 1,266-metre Mt. Odake. If you want extra vertical, skip the Mitake cable car and climb the steep cedar switchbacks from Takimoto; if you want more time on the upper ridge, ride the six-minute funicular to 831 metres and warm up past Musashi-Mitake Shrine.

Instead of retracing your steps to the JR Ome Line, drop down Odake's quieter southern flank directly into Hinode Town's Tsurutsuru Onsen. Pumped from 1,500 metres underground, its high-alkaline pH 10.1 spring water gives the facility its name (tsuru-tsuru means silky-smooth). From the bathhouse entrance, a direct local bus drops you at JR Musashi-Itsukaichi Station for the ride back to Shinjuku.

Bottom lineVaried terrain, mild summit scrambling, and one of West Tokyo's best alkaline valley baths.
03Best pure vertical conditioning (+1,200 m)

3. Mt. Tonodake (1,491 m) via Tanzawa's Okura Ridge

Train & bus accessOdakyu Line from Shinjuku → Shibusawa Station (~75 min), then 15 min bus from North Exit #2 to Okura
Distance & vertical14.0 km out-and-back · +1,200 m relentless gain · 5.5 to 6.5 hours
Finish-line onsenTsurumaki Onsen Kobo-no-Satoyu (10:00–21:00, ¥800 weekday / ¥1,000 weekend for 2h; 2 stops east on Odakyu)
Watch out forAvoid humid rainy days from June to September when Tanzawa's lower valleys host land leeches (yamabiru); Kobo-no-Satoyu shuts Mondays

Japanese alpinists affectionately call the seven-kilometre climb from Okura bus terminal to Mt. Tonodake the Baka-One ("Idiot's Ridge") because it never flattens out: just 1,200 vertical metres of wooden staircases, beech roots and volcanic gravel straight up to 1,491 metres. If you are preparing for Mt. Fuji, the Northern Japanese Alps or a summer four-thousander in Europe, there is no better fitness benchmark within ninety minutes of Shinjuku.

Pick a crisp November-to-April morning when Mt. Fuji towers cloudless across the valley from Sonobutsu Hut at the summit, and pack trekking poles to save your patellar tendons on the 1,200-metre descent. Back at Shibusawa Station, ride the Odakyu Line ten minutes east to Tsurumaki-Onsen Station, where municipal bathhouse Kobo-no-Satoyu sits a two-minute walk from the ticket gate with high-calcium natural spring tubs.

Bottom lineTokyo's premier 1,200-metre vertical leg workout, paired with an ¥800–¥1,000 station-side mineral soak.
04Best waterfall gorge & craft beer finish

4. Mt. Kawanori (1,363 m) via Hyakuhiro Falls to Okutama Station

Train & bus accessJR Chuo/Ome Special Rapid (Holiday Rapid Okutama on weekends) Shinjuku → Okutama (~105 min) + 15 min bus to Kawanori-bashi
Distance & vertical13.5 km traverse · +1,050 m ascent · 5.5 to 6.5 hours (descend to Hatonosu or Okutama)
Finish-line onsenOkutama Onsen Moegi-no-Yu (10:00–20:00 Apr–Nov / 19:00 Dec–Mar, ¥1,050 for 3h; 10 min walk from JR Okutama)
Watch out forMoegi-no-Yu closes Mondays (Tuesday if Monday is a national holiday) and puts up entry waitlists around 15:30–17:00 in autumn foliage season

Starting from Kawanori-bashi bus stop, this route follows narrow steel footbridges up a deep limestone stream valley to Hyakuhiro Falls, a forty-metre cascade tucked into old-growth forest, before grinding steeply onto the quiet 1,363-metre summit of Mt. Kawanori. Compared with Takao or Mitake, foot traffic drops sharply once you climb above the waterfall.

Descend south-east along the ridge to Hatonosu Station (two stops from Okutama) or walk right back into Okutama village for Moegi-no-Yu, whose cedar outdoor bath looks straight down into the turquoise Tama River gorge. Bonus recovery stop: independent taproom Vertere sits right across from JR Okutama Station if you want a fresh local pale ale before boarding the Holiday Rapid back to Shinjuku.

Bottom lineWilder Okutama gorge scenery than Mitake, ending at a riverside outdoor bath and Tokyo's best trailhead taproom.
05Best half-day steep climb & shrine stairs

5. Mt. Oyama (1,252 m) Full Climb via Afuri Shrine

Train & bus accessOdakyu Rapid Express from Shinjuku → Isehara Station (~60 min), then 25 min bus from North Exit #4 to Oyama Cable
Distance & vertical8.4 km loop · +940 m on foot from bus stop (+650 m if using cable car) · 4 to 5 hours
Finish-line onsenTsurumaki Onsen Kobo-no-Satoyu (1 stop west from Isehara on Odakyu Line; ¥800–¥1,000, 10:00–21:00)
Watch out forStone stairs on the Men's Trail (Otoko-zaka) turn slick after rain; Kobo-no-Satoyu is closed Mondays

Rising as an isolated pyramid at the eastern edge of Tanzawa, Mt. Oyama packs nearly a vertical kilometre into a compact 8.4-kilometre loop. Skip the tourist cable car and climb the steep stone steps of the Otoko-zaka (Men's Trail) to Oyama Afuri Shrine Shimosha at 700 metres, then push straight up through giant cedar roots to the 1,252-metre summit looking out over Sagami Bay and Enoshima.

Descend the eastern Miharashi Ridge to form a clean loop that spares your knees from repeating the steepest stone slabs. Because Isehara sits just four minutes by train from Tsurumaki-Onsen Station, you can be soaking in calcium-chloride hot spring water less than forty minutes after stepping off the trailhead bus.

Bottom lineFastest way from Shinjuku to nearly 1,000 metres of steep stair conditioning.
06Best winter snow-free coastal adventure

6. Mt. Nokogiriyama (329 m) Quarry Walls via Tokyo Bay Ferry

Train & ferry accessKeikyu Line from Shinagawa → Keikyu Kurihama (~65 min) + bus to Kurihama Port + 40 min Tokyo-Wan Ferry (¥1,100) to Kanaya
Distance & vertical7.5 km loop via Shachoku / Kanto Fureai Trail & Nihon-ji (¥700 temple fee) · +480 m · 3.5 to 4.5 hours
Finish-line onsenNatural Onsen Umibe-no-Yu (10:00–20:30 wkday / 09:00–21:00 wkend, ¥1,100–¥1,200; 12 min walk south of Kanaya Port)
Watch out forNihon-ji Temple gates shut early (16:00 in winter, 17:00 summer), and strong gale winds across Uraga Channel can suspend the ferry (JR Uchibo Line at Hamakanaya is your rail backup)

When January and February coat Okutama and Tanzawa ridges in hard trail ice, look south across Tokyo Bay to the Boso Peninsula. Leaving Shinagawa on the Keikyu Line and crossing the Uraga Channel on the Tokyo-Wan Ferry turns a standard Sunday hike into a proper micro-expedition before your boots even touch dirt at Kanaya Port.

Despite its modest 329-metre summit, Mt. Nokogiriyama climbs sharply through vertical Edo-period stone quarry walls, narrow rock corridors, the overhanging Jigoku-Nozoki cliff lookout and thousands of stone steps across Nihon-ji's hillside precinct. Walk ten minutes down the coast road afterwards to Umibe-no-Yu, where dark amber mineral baths look straight back across the water toward Miura and Mt. Fuji at sunset.

Bottom lineZero ice in mid-winter, dramatic vertical stone quarries, and a ferry-plus-seaside-onsen return.

Two small kit habits make train-to-onsen days smoother: pack a 30-gram microfibre face towel and a sealed drybag for your sweat-soaked base layer so your pack does not smell on the Chuo Line home, and keep a ¥1,000 note plus ¥100 coins in a zip pocket for rural unmanned lockers and trailhead bath vending machines.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to reserve train tickets in advance for Okutama or Tanzawa day hikes?

No. Standard commuter trains on the JR Chuo/Ome Line (including the weekend Holiday Rapid Okutama), Keio Line, Odakyu Line and Keikyu Line run on tap-and-go Suica or PASMO IC cards with no reservation needed. The only optional upgrade is booking a reserved Odakyu Romancecar seat back from Isehara or Hadano if you want guaranteed legroom after a long climb.

Which Tokyo mountain day hikes stay snow-free in January and February?

Mt. Nokogiriyama on Chiba's Boso Peninsula stays snow-free all winter, and the Mt. Jinba to Mt. Takao ridge usually stays dry or requires only light microspikes after rare cold snaps. Above 1,200 metres — including Mt. Tonodake, Mt. Kawanori and Mt. Odake's shady northern gullies — packed snow and hard freeze-thaw trail ice are common from late December through early March, so carry 6-point or chain microspikes.

Do trailhead hot springs like Moegi-no-Yu or Gokurakuyu allow tattoos?

Most municipal and suburban trailhead bathhouses around Tokyo (including Keio Takaosan Onsen Gokurakuyu, Moegi-no-Yu and Tsurutsuru Onsen) still enforce traditional no-tattoo rules, though small discrete marks fully hidden under a skin-toned waterproof patch rarely draw attention at quiet village baths unless explicitly prohibited at check-in. If you have larger visible ink, ride the train back into Shinjuku or Haneda and use one of Tokyo's tattoo-friendly or sticker-approved super sento instead.

What navigation app works best offline on Okutama and Tanzawa trails?

Download YAMAP (Japan's standard mountain GPS app) and save the offline map for Takao/Jinba, Okutama or Tanzawa before leaving Shinjuku. Google Maps omits key ridge junctions, seasonal bridge washouts and water-source notes, whereas YAMAP tracks your live offline GPS position even in deep cedar valleys with zero mobile signal.
