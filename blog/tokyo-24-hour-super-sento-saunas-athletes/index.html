Tokyo: 6 best 24-hour super sento, saunas & cold plunges after a run or hike

Where to go in Tokyo when you step off a late mountain train at Shinjuku, finish a long loop around the Imperial Palace, or land on a red-eye at Haneda. Real hours, bath-cleaning blackouts, cold-plunge quality, yen prices and exact tattoo rules.

Most English guides to Tokyo bathhouses treat every complex as a tourist novelty. If you live and train here, or you are flying through Tokyo with a climbing duffel, you judge a super sento on four much simpler things: how cold the mizuburo plunge actually is, whether the location lines up with a train terminal or running route, when the baths shut for overnight cleaning, and whether the front desk will turn you away for a small tattoo.

A quick definition before the list: a traditional neighbourhood sento is a ¥550 local bathhouse where you bring your own soap and leave in forty minutes. A super sento is a full recovery complex — natural mineral or carbonated baths, dry and steam saunas above 85°C, cold plunges, luggage-sized lockers, restaurant floors that serve high-protein meals at midnight, and reclining nap rooms that cost a third of a capsule hotel if you miss the last train.

01Best post-hike Shinjuku hub

1. Shinjuku Natural Onsen Thermae-Yu (Kabukicho)

Station access2 min from Higashi-Shinjuku / Shinjuku-sanchome; 9 min walk from JR Shinjuku
Entry & overnight¥3,100 weekday / ¥3,200 weekend; +¥1,800–¥2,300 after midnight
Recovery highlightHigh-concentration carbonated bath + auto-löyly dry sauna & cold plunge
Watch out forMain pools close briefly around 03:00–06:00 for cleaning while lounges stay open

Nearly every serious day hike west of Tokyo — Okutama ridges on the JR Ome Line, Mt. Mitake, or Tanzawa traverses on the Odakyu Line — dumps you back at Shinjuku Station with stiff quads and salt on your shell. Thermae-Yu sits ten minutes from the platform, stays open 24 hours a day, and hands you towels and loungewear at check-in so you do not need to carry extra kit in your daypack.

The standout recovery tub here is the large indoor high-concentration carbonated bath (tansansen). Kept lukewarm around 38°C rather than scalding hot, dissolved CO2 dilates peripheral blood vessels without spiking your heart rate the way a 43°C tub does after five hours on a ridge. Pair two rounds of the high-heat sauna and cold plunge with fifteen minutes in the carbonated pool and your legs feel noticeably lighter the next morning.

Bottom lineUnbeatable logistics if your mountain train terminates at Shinjuku or you miss the last Yamanote train.
02Best central sauna & mineral spring

2. Tokyo Dome Natural Onsen Spa LaQua (Bunkyo)

Station access1–3 min from Korakuen / Suidobashi / Kasuga Stations
Entry & overnight¥3,200–¥4,100 (¥1,000 evening discount after 19:00; +¥2,500 past 01:00)
Recovery highlight1,700m-deep natural sodium-chloride amber spring + 4 distinct saunas
Watch out forZero-tolerance tattoo ban (even small covered tattoos are refused)

Unlike central bathhouses that truck spring water in from outside Tokyo, Spa LaQua pumps amber-coloured fossil seawater from 1,700 metres directly beneath Tokyo Dome City. The high salt content holds core warmth long after you towel off, and the men's bath zone alone runs multiple sauna temperatures (from an 80°C Finnish-style room with self-löyly up to a 100°C dry sauna) alongside two cold plunges at ~17°C and ~22°C.

For Tokyo runners, the real trick is timing: Suidobashi sits a short jog north of the 5 km Imperial Palace (Kokyo) loop. Checking in after 19:00 triggers Spa LaQua's ¥1,000 evening discount, turning a weekday tempo run into a two-hour sauna and mineral-bath session before dinner.

Bottom lineThe highest-spec bath and sauna hardware in central Tokyo, provided you have zero ink.
03Best bayfront run + sunrise soak

3. Tokyo Toyosu Manyo Club (Toyosu)

Station accessDirectly beside Shijo-mae Station (Yurikamome Line)
Entry & overnight¥4,000 + ¥150 tax (¥2,200 morning bath 06:00–09:00; +¥3,000 past 03:00)
Recovery highlightOpen-air baths overlooking Tokyo Bay + 360° rooftop footbath
Watch out forPriciest standard daytime ticket unless you use the ¥2,200 early-morning window

Opened alongside Toyosu Senkyaku Banrai, Manyo Club trucks natural spring water daily from Hakone and Yugawara into high-floor open-air stone baths looking straight across Tokyo Bay. Because Toyosu sits surrounded by flat waterfront boardwalks — including the 4.5 km Toyosu Gururi Park path where marathoners dial in uninterrupted threshold pace with zero traffic lights — it makes an exceptional weekend long-run finish line.

If ¥4,150 feels steep for a quick soak, look at the 06:00 to 09:00 morning bath ticket (¥2,200). Running 15–20 km around Tokyo Bay at dawn and stepping into an empty open-air bath at 07:00 is one of the sharpest ways to start a Saturday in the city. Small tattoos are accepted if fully hidden beneath up to two facility-designated 11 × 20 cm cover stickers.

Bottom linePair the flat Toyosu Gururi Park waterfront run with the ¥2,200 morning bath window.
04Best tattoo-friendly & transit reset

4. Izumi Tenku no Yu Haneda Airport (Terminal 3)

Station accessDirect indoor walkway from Haneda Airport Terminal 3 (12F)
Entry & overnight¥4,800 towels & loungewear included (+¥4,000 late surcharge 01:00–05:00)
Recovery highlightOpen-air natural chloride spring with runway & clear-day Mt. Fuji views
Watch out forBaths close daily 10:00–12:30 for cleaning; steepest overnight surcharge

Twelve hours cramped in economy class after an alpine trip leaves your ankles swollen and your lower back locked up. Sitting on the roof of Villa Fontaine Grand directly connected to Haneda Terminal 3, Izumi Tenku no Yu is the single most convenient pre- or post-flight recovery stop in Japan — and, critically for international climbers and runners, it is genuinely tattoo-friendly without requiring cover stickers.

Both the dry sauna and the outdoor natural hot spring look out across Haneda's runways (and across to Mt. Fuji on crisp winter mornings). Just watch the clock on daytime layovers: the baths shut strictly from 10:00 to 12:30 every morning for deep cleaning, even though the relaxation lounges remain open.

Bottom lineWorth every yen before a long-haul night flight or when travelling with tattooed climbing partners.
05Best budget 24h airport sleeper

5. Natural Hot Spring Heiwajima (Ota Ward)

Station access¥100 shuttle from JR Omori or Keikyu Heiwajima; direct Haneda flight buses
Entry & overnight¥2,400–¥2,900 standard (7h); ¥5,000 all-in Haneda Flight/Welcome bus package
Recovery highlight150 tons/day of high-salinity natural spring water + high-heat löyly sauna
Watch out forOlder retro mall building aesthetic compared with sleek new Toyosu or Haneda T3

If you are catching a 06:15 Skymark or AIRDO morning flight out of Haneda to Sapporo for a Hokkaidō ski weekend — or landing at 23:45 — paying ¥8,800 overnight at Terminal 3 is hard to justify. Ten minutes north in Ota Ward, Natural Hot Spring Heiwajima has quietly solved that exact problem for decades.

Their ¥5,000 Flight & Welcome courses bundle overnight recliner lounge access, towels, spa wear, and a reserved seat on dedicated 03:00–06:00 early-morning or post-midnight shuttle buses straight to Haneda Terminal 3. Up to four small tattoos can be covered using ¥260 stickers sold at reception.

Bottom lineThe smartest ¥5,000 overnight launchpad for early-morning domestic ski and hiking flights out of Haneda.
06Best pure value & carbonated cold plunge

6. Spadium Japon — 'Spajapo' (Higashikurume)

Station accessFree shuttle bus from Tanashi (Seibu-Shinjuku) or Higashi-Kurume (Seibu-Ikebukuro)
Entry & overnight¥850 weekday / ¥950 weekend (+¥850–¥950 bedrock lounge; closes 01:00–02:00)
Recovery highlightSparkling carbonated cold plunge (~15°C) beside a steam-injected sauna
Watch out forNot 24 hours (closes 01:00–02:00); bring your own towel or pay small rental

Included as the one non-24-hour exception because no recovery list around Tokyo is complete without it: Spadium Japon in West Tokyo charges under ¥1,000 for weekday bath admission, taps a clear natural sodium-bicarbonate spring across 15 pools, and runs something you almost never see outside specialist sauna clubs — a carbonated cold plunge alongside an icy ~13°C single-person plunge tub.

Carbonated cold water strips the thin insulating boundary layer off your skin immediately, making 15°C feel several degrees colder without numbing joints. Avoid rainy Sunday afternoons when the 30,000-manga bedrock terrace packs out with students; go on a weekday morning or late after 21:30 for quiet laps between sauna and cold water.

Bottom lineBest sub-¥1,000 sauna and dual cold-plunge hardware anywhere in Greater Tokyo.

One practical habit across all six: rehydrate with 500 ml of water + electrolytes before your second sauna round, not after you walk out. Alternating 90°C heat and 15°C cold right after a two-hour run or mountain descent drops blood pressure fast if you arrive already dehydrated.

Frequently asked questions

Can you actually sleep overnight at a 24-hour super sento in Tokyo instead of booking a hotel?

Yes — at Thermae-Yu, Spa LaQua, Toyosu Manyo Club, Izumi Tenku no Yu (Haneda T3) and Heiwajima, paying the late-night surcharge lets you sleep in quiet dimmed reclining chairs (each with reading lights and USB/AC outlets) until morning. Just bring earplugs, and note that bathing pools close for 1.5 to 3 hours during overnight or mid-morning cleaning.

Which Tokyo super sento allows tattoos without cover stickers?

Among Tokyo's major modern super sento complexes, Izumi Tenku no Yu at Haneda Airport Terminal 3 permits uncovered tattoos inside all baths and saunas. Toyosu Manyo Club, Heiwajima, Thermae-Yu and Spadium Japon only allow small tattoos that fit completely under designated waterproof cover stickers, while Spa LaQua enforces a strict zero-tattoo rule.

Do I need to pack towels, shampoo, or change of clothes in my running or hiking pack?

At the five full-service complexes (Thermae-Yu, Spa LaQua, Toyosu Manyo Club, Haneda T3, and Heiwajima), face towels, bath towels, loungewear, shampoo, body wash and hairdryers are included in standard entry. Only budget-priced Spadium Japon (¥850–¥950) charges separately for towel rental, so you can bring an ultralight microfibre towel there to save yen.

Can I store a large hiking backpack or ski roller bag at a Tokyo super sento?

Yes. Haneda T3, Toyosu Manyo Club, Heiwajima and Thermae-Yu all provide either oversized suitcase lockers near check-in or front-desk baggage storage tags for bulky rucksacks that do not fit narrow changing-room lockers.
