# Adventure Notes

## 2026-07-18

Sure. Let me tell you about my motorbike trip across Vietnam.
It started in August 2013. I had just finished the Camino de Santiago, and I had to go to Vietnam for start working in a French company called Havas Media as an account director. I was 23, had no experience in Vietnam.
I had a dream to motorcycle across Vietnam, as my work was only starting in September, and I had roughly 40 days ahead of me.
I landed in Ho Chi Minh, went to backpacker street, took a shady room paid by the day. I then caught up with a friend, Riton de Poudenas, who was already living there. He was awesome, a real, strong, red head, who knew how to ride a motorbike.
He taught me how to ride one, one evening on a street. Then I went to Bui Vien, the backpacker street where I was renting a room, and found a motorbike shop. I purchased an old, Honda Win 110 copy, and the day after, I was riding into the distance on an early morning to avoid rush hour. I had never ridden a bike before, had no idea where I would sleep that night, but I loved the adventure.
I quickly got to understand how to ride the bike, switch gear, with the short knowledge I had from learning from Riton (his real name is Henry, Riton is a nickname).
I headed towards Buon Ma Thuot. I thought I could stop by the side of the road in the evening, and ask locals to sleep in their home. And so I did.
On that first evening, after Buon Ma Thuot, I stopped by the side of the road, in a poor house of a family whose job was to cut open cashew nuts with a small device with blades. It was rough, but they were so welcoming. We cheered and drank and they let me sleep in their home. The next day I thanked them, offered to pay, and went off to continue my road.
I knew I could also count on the generosity of the Catholic churches in the highlands. So I kept going towards Pleiku and Kontum, where I did meet Catholic communities. But this is for another story.
